Her chest tightened as she opened the letter. The words blurred. Her heart
hammered against her ribs and a knot formed in the pit of her stomach.

She had spent eleven years at the firm. Eleven years of early trains and late
dinners and the slow accumulation of a life she had not chosen so much as
agreed to, one Tuesday at a time.

She swallowed hard. Her hands trembled as she folded the paper along its
original creases and slid it back into the envelope.

The lesson here is that we do not get to pick our endings. Looking back, I
realized that the things I had been most afraid of were the ones that finally
set me free. In the end, the letter was not the ending at all.
